- Next event: 21:25:08.805 UTC, price 1.58700000, market event ID 1119.
- Gap: 3,242.118 seconds (54m 2.118s).

Both symbols' `aggTrade` subscriptions were confirmed during startup. The afternoon runtime contains no logged market-stream error or reconnect transition explaining this ZRO interval. ZRO user-data fills were nevertheless recorded during the broader session.

### Source-level context

The current implementation adds useful safety checks, but not proactive per-symbol liveness detection:

- `BinanceWebSocketBroker.subscribeForPrices()` subscribes to the symbol's `aggTrade` stream. `adaptAggTrades()` maps Binance `T` (trade time) to normalized `timestamp` and `E` (event time) to `eventTime`.
- The Streams client confirms initial subscriptions with `LIST_SUBSCRIPTIONS`, retains desired listeners, and reconnects after an observed transport close. It does not expose per-symbol last-message age, stream-level heartbeat, or a silent-logical-stream watchdog.
- Harvester's market-price intake retains only whether a fresh initial WebSocket price was seen and the latest processing promise. It does not publish last-receipt age or stale/recovered transitions.
- The domain freshness helper rejects price events older than 5,000 ms (default) and allows 1,000 ms future skew. The engine also checks price freshness when evaluating whether an order may be submitted. These checks prevent a decision from using an already-stale cached price, but they do not proactively report a stream that has stopped delivering.
- Trading mode also subscribes to per-symbol `bookTicker`. This is a second logical stream on the same public Streams connection, not an independent transport. The adapter assigns it a local `Date.now()` receipt timestamp.
- Both supplied runtime logs record that DOGEUSDT and ZROUSDT bookTicker subscriptions were established at 16:33:40.590 and 16:33:41.190 UTC (main log lines 9719 and 9721; TRACE lines 11523 and 11526). Neither log records bookTicker update receipts. Source inspection of `apps/harvester/src/bootstrap/subscriptions/subscribe-to-market-data.js` shows that successful bookTicker callbacks are forwarded to `processMarketBook()` without a normal receipt log; only processing failures are logged. Consequently, the absence of bookTicker receipt lines is expected from current instrumentation and is not evidence of a stalled bookTicker stream. The uploaded files cannot retrospectively establish whether ZRO bookTicker continued updating during the aggTrade gap.

The implementation trace is recorded in [#760](https://github.com/45s/coinzilla/issues/760). The 54-minute aggTrade gap remains ambiguous: it may be low/no trade activity or a silent per-symbol delivery stall. Do not classify it as a confirmed Binance outage.

### What is and is not established

Established:
- ZRO market-price events were materially less frequent than DOGE events in this session.
- One ZRO inter-event interval exceeded 54 minutes.
- The application continued processing DOGE events and other work during that interval.
- No afternoon market-stream error was logged.

Not established:
- That Binance stopped publishing ZRO aggregate trades.
- That the ZRO stream disconnected or its subscription was lost.
- That a price decision was made using stale ZRO data.
- That the market was expected to produce trades more frequently during the interval.

An `aggTrade` stream emits on trades; low activity is a possible explanation. The supplied files do not contain an independent exchange feed or a separate per-symbol heartbeat against which to validate the interval. Keep this as an anomaly until independently explained.

### Recommended follow-up

Issue #760 requires a focused investigation and bounded per-symbol observability: desired/confirmed subscription state, last received event time, event age, reconnect generation, and stale/recovered transitions. Determine whether book-ticker freshness or another documented exchange signal can distinguish quiet trading from a silent stream. Any trading pause/gating policy must be explicitly specified and tested; do not invent order mutations or a global halt as part of diagnostics.

## 6. Finding F-02 — repeated duplicate-fill batches during shutdown

**Classification:** SOURCE-EXPLAINED / LATENCY STRONGLY CORRELATED  
**Severity:** Medium operational concern  
**Tracking:** [#766](https://github.com/45s/coinzilla/issues/766)

Shutdown began at 22:38:29.849 UTC. The runtime then processed two large batches of duplicate fill observations:

- Around 22:40:15 UTC: 48 duplicate notifications.
- Around 22:41:59 UTC: another 48 duplicate notifications.

Both batches refer to the same 48 already-applied fill identities. Runtime accounting reports `applied: false`, `duplicate: true`, and zero accounting delta for those observations. The Journal then records a complete CLOSING account snapshot at 22:42:00.445 UTC, broker disconnection at 22:42:05.490 UTC, and `harvester.stopped` with `reason: SIGINT`, `outcome: success` at 22:42:05.491 UTC.

### Reconstructed owned-order set

The Journal records 90 unique submitted orders:

- 40 unique orders were canceled without any fill.
- 48 unique orders have applied fills; their latest Journal order updates report `FILLED`.
- The remaining two submitted orders are OPEN SELLs with zero executed quantity: DOGEUSDT broker order `1333893` and ZROUSDT broker order `756212`.

Thus 50 owned orders remained in the in-process registry at shutdown: 48 FILLED orders plus two OPEN SELL orders. The two SELLs are preserved by shutdown policy and are not cancellation targets.

### Source and timing correlation

The current source explains both duplicate batches and strongly correlates the approximately 105-second waits with sequential REST reconciliation:

1. `create-shutdown-handler.js` invokes `reconcileOwnedOrders()` before owned BUY cancellation and again after cancellation/user-data queue drain. Both passes are required safety checks.
2. Each call invokes `collectOwnedOrderSnapshots()`. It first fetches account open orders once per distinct symbol (2 requests).
3. It then iterates all 50 owned orders sequentially, fetching canonical order history once per order (50 requests).
4. It iterates the same 50 orders sequentially again, fetching complete fill history once per order (50 requests in this session; all results fit within one page).

The source-level RPC count is 202 sequential broker REST requests per pass, or approximately 404 across both passes: 2 symbol-level open-orders calls; 50 canonical-history calls, each doing `allOrders` + `exchangeInfo`; and 50 fill-history pages, each doing `exchangeInfo` + `myTrades`. The first duplicate-fill batch appears after the first complete snapshot collection; the second appears after the second collection. The second pass replays the same 48 fill identities through normal idempotent accounting, which explains the duplicate notifications without double application.

The first batch began about 105 seconds after shutdown started; the second began about 104 seconds after the first. The corrected 202 sequential RPCs per pass strongly correlate with this timing (about 0.52 seconds per RPC if evenly distributed), but exact per-request latency is not directly measured in the supplied logs. The code currently has no per-stage/per-RPC duration records, so this remains a correlation rather than measured latency attribution.

### Required follow-up

[#766](https://github.com/45s/coinzilla/issues/766) tracks bounded shutdown-stage and REST-call timing, followed by a careful evaluation of bounded concurrency/batching for independent history reads. Preserve both reconciliation passes, complete fill history, pagination/completeness checks, and fail-closed behavior. Do not cancel the two open SELLs, skip filled orders, or weaken accounting safety to shorten shutdown.

## Additional recovery finding — safety-halt reconciliation may mutate recovery SELLs

Current-source review found that the account-integrity safety halt calls `reconcileOwnedOrders()` with no options. The engine defaults this to `recoveryMode=false`, so applying a recovered FILLED BUY can initiate recovery SELL cancellation before the safety halt reaches its explicit BUY-only cancellation stage.

The 03:22:24 UTC ZRO cancellation for recovery SELL `749343` has a stack through `applyOwnedOrderSnapshotsTransition()` and is immediately followed by the safety-halt reconciliation-failed log. This strongly correlates with the safety-halt's own reconciliation pass, but does not prove caller attribution from the trace alone. See F-024 in `AUDIT_FINDINGS.md` and [#773](https://github.com/45s/coinzilla/issues/773). The existing unit tests cover recovery-mode reconciliation and BUY-only cancellation separately, but not the complete safety-halt workflow.


Current source initializes `accountingFailed=false` only once in `start-harvester.js`; the inspected bootstrap has no reset-to-false path. The status handler will not clear `requiresReconciliation` after broker acknowledgement while this latch is true, and the recovery coordinator refuses all retries. The broad latch is therefore process-lifetime terminal, not a temporary backoff. This may be appropriate for true accounting corruption but can strand recovery after a downstream cancellation failure; see F-023/#769.

Current `develop` contains separate recovery-mode protections, but still has the F-023 broad error latch, F-024 safety-halt default-mode risk, and F-025 NOT_SENT/UNKNOWN classification issue.


## Additional market-data source finding — error without close

Current public Streams code routes a transport error to a diagnostic observer only; only a close event marks the client closed and schedules reconnect. The broker's market-data observer logs the error but does not update per-stream health. Thus a post-open error without a subsequent close can leave the connection reported as open without an automatic reconnect or stale transition. This is a current liveness gap, not an explanation for the November 8 ZRO gap: no afternoon market-stream error was logged. See F-026 and [#760](https://github.com/45s/coinzilla/issues/760). Future work should distinguish fatal transport errors from nonfatal observer/message errors and add bounded per-stream receipt-age monitoring, not unconditional reconnect.
